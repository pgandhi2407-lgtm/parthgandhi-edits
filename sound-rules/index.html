My sound rules: what goes where.

By Parth Gandhi, video editor

AI can build an edit that's clean and on time and still feels dead. Sound is the part I add myself. These are the rules I follow, each with the real moment from my reel so you can hear it.

The rule behind all of them: a sound should mean something. Every whoosh says "new scene", every click says "look here". If a sound doesn't mean anything, leave it out.

The five sounds

Tap a clip to hear it with sound.
Whoosh

When the scene moves.

A whoosh tells the viewer "new idea". Put one on every real change of scene: a wipe, a camera move, a new section. Not on every word that animates.

In my reel: The red wipe into "4 edit styles".
Click

When a box or highlight lands on a word.

The click is the sound of emphasis. It goes on the frame the red box finishes growing, so the eye and ear agree on the same word.

In my reel: The red box on "didn't".
Hit

For every item in a list.

One hit per item, same sound each time. The repetition is the point: it turns a list into a rhythm the viewer can count.

In my reel: Each note card flipping in.
Thud

When something big settles into place.

A low thud gives weight. Use it when a group of things lands, or on the final click of a button, so the moment feels finished.

In my reel: The references landing in the ring.
Tick

On one word you want them to notice.

A tiny, bright tick is for a single word. Use it once or twice in a reel at most, or it stops meaning anything.

In my reel: "this reel" in the hook.

Three mixing rules

  1. Sound sits under the voice, never over it.If you notice the SFX before you notice what I'm saying, it's too loud.
  2. Line it up with the frame, not the clip.Put the peak of the sound on the exact frame the motion lands, not where the sound file starts.
  3. Leave some moves silent.If every animation makes a noise, none of them matter. Silence is what makes the important ones land.

Music and transitions

Music sets the pace, it doesn't fight the voice. Keep it low under speech and let it come up in the gaps. Pick it for tempo first, mood second.

A transition is for a new idea, not a new sentence. If the thought hasn't changed, a straight cut is cleaner. Save transitions for where the reel actually turns a corner.

One last thing

AI can build the edit. The feel is still the job. Sound is the quickest way to put yours into it.
